Understanding the Challenge

What NASA's material shows

  • Artemis II A crewed mission where the rocket and the spacecraft have distinct jobs.
  • Mission Control Center Specialized ground roles. One voice, Capcom, talks to the crew.
  • Rocket Systems Area Equipment is checked before it is trusted.
  • Missions, A to Z A mission directory and an alphabetical research index.

The problem we identified

Real missions require specialized teams, detailed procedures and crew-ground coordination.

We chose to make that information-sharing relationship something players can experience together.

What a newcomer needs

An approachable way to practice reporting clearly and acting on an instruction, together.

Our goal: make that coordination playable.

NASA sources Artemis II · Missions · A to Z · Rocket Systems Area · Mission Control Center

This is our design rationale from reading these sources. We have not run a study with users.

Prototype

Godot 4.7.2 · GDScript · ENet

One player hosts and validates every action. Each role receives only its own view.

Testing

59.8–59.9 average FPS

Two local processes on one integrated-graphics laptop, 1280×720. Automated checks run on Windows and Linux.

Still pending: separate networks and tests with players.

AI assistance OpenAI Codex/ChatGPT and Anthropic Claude/Claude Code helped with code, scene design, checks and documentation. The team chose the direction and reviewed the result.

The puzzles and the countdown are game rules we wrote. They are not NASA procedures.

Our Solution: Two Roles, One Mission

“The antenna light is fixed.”

“Fixed light: select the Moon.”

Host validates. Antenna ready.

Prepare → Observe → Communicate → Act → Receive feedback

Implemented in the prototype

Lobby and role selection · Easy and normal difficulty · Antenna and numeric-code modules · Manual and calculator · Shared countdown · Liftoff, timeout and interrupted outcomes

Proposed next

Tests with players · Play across separate networks · More modules · A packaged Windows build

What we expect players to take away: cooperation, clear communication, and a feel for how a mission is run. It is an educational adaptation, not an exact simulation.
